## 1. Why this topic

Desertification is defined by the UN Convention to Combat Desertification as "land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas resulting from various factors, including climatic variations and human activities", where those drylands are "areas, other than polar and sub-polar regions, in which the ratio of annual precipitation to potential evapotranspiration falls within the range from 0.05 to 0.65" ([UNCCD Convention text, Art. 1](https://www.unccd.int/sites/default/files/relevant-links/2017-01/UNCCD_Convention_ENG_0.pdf)). Measuring it is an explicit global policy target: UN Sustainable Development Goal indicator **15.3.1, "Proportion of land that is degraded over total land area"**, is computed from three sub-indicators, "Trends in Land Cover, Land Productivity and Carbon Stocks" ([SDG 15.3.1 metadata, UN Statistics Division](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-15-03-01.pdf)). Land productivity in particular is usually tracked from satellite vegetation signals, which is exactly what free Sentinel-2 imagery provides at 10–20 m, far finer than the coarse sensors most degradation trend studies rely on. A data-mining approach can help separate degradation from the strong year-to-year swings caused by rainfall, which is the hard part of the problem.

### **Personal Motivations**

As climate change continues to accelerate and ravage our natural world, I believe we need to use all the tools available to us to study and better understand the phenomena which most impact Earth's ability to sustain life. Desertification is very troubling; it not only saps total arable land in affected areas but also reduces a given area's ability to sequester carbon, in effect accelerating the impacts of human carbon emissions. I have long been interested in remote sensing, and this project is an excellent opportunity to combine my interests in environmental justice and satellite imagery analysis. I hope this project can serve as a proof of concept of what is possible when modern machine learning techniques are applied to environmental studies.

## 5. Comparison and choice

| | Sentinel Hub on CDSE | Planetary Computer STAC |
|---|---|---|
| Account needed | Yes (free CDSE account + OAuth client) | No |
| Quota | 10 000 PU and 10 000 requests per month (general tier) | None published; throttled |
| Server-side processing | Yes (evalscripts, cloud masking, compositing, statistics) | No (download files, process locally) |
| Sentinel-2 L2A | Yes, harmonised across processing baselines | Yes |
| Long history | Sentinel-2 only (2015+) | Landsat from 1982 |
| Auxiliary data | DEM 90 m (30 m restricted) | WorldCover, DEM 30 m |
| Service status | Production service with published quotas | "Preview", no service-level commitment |

**Decision:** keep **Sentinel Hub on CDSE as the primary source** for Sentinel-2 L2A. Server-side compositing and cloud masking keep downloads small enough to fit the quota, and values are harmonised across the 2022 processing-baseline change. **Planetary Computer is the secondary source**: it supplies the open 30 m DEM and WorldCover, it is a fallback for Sentinel-2 if the quota runs out, and Landsat gives optional long-term context.

**Feasibility of size (at least 10 000 records with 15 attributes):** one 20 km × 20 km area gridded into 100 m cells gives 40 000 cells; with 7 years × 2 seasons that is 560 000 cell-season records, each with 10 band values plus 9 indices plus auxiliary variables (ROADMAP §4.3–§4.4). The exact numbers depend on the final study areas (M1-T02, M1-T03).

## 6. Implications for the rest of the project

1. **One month's quota is the whole budget.** Quotas reset monthly, but the project is planned to finish within one month, so all Sentinel Hub use must fit in our 30 000 PU / 30 000 request allowance. Usage is logged per batch and the scope (study areas, years, resolution, bands) is reduced if needed.
2. **Composites cost more than single images.** PU cost multiplies by the number of data samples per pixel and doubles for 32-bit float output, so a seasonal median over many acquisitions is much more expensive than one scene. The pilot (M1-T05) must measure this before scaling up; options are 16-bit output, fewer bands and coarser resolution.
3. **DEM from Planetary Computer.** Use `cop-dem-glo-30` from Planetary Computer, since COPERNICUS_30 on CDSE needs authorised access.
4. **CHIRPS v3, not v2.** v2 production ends after December 2026; v3 also covers 60°N–60°S, enough for every candidate study area.

## 7. Study areas (M1-T02)

*Final AOIs (`config/aois.geojson`), confirmed by the project owner on 2026-11-21 after the climate, availability and imagery checks in `notebooks/01b_aoi_selection.ipynb`.*

Six candidate areas of about 20 km × 20 km each cover the dynamics we want to separate: degradation (Aral Sea, Lake Urmia), mixed rainfall- and land-use-driven change (two Sahel sites), re-greening (Kubuqi) and a stable hyper-arid control (an erg in the Sahara) (`reports/figures/m1_aoi_map.png`). Each AOI is a square in the UTM zone of its centre with edges on the 100 m cell grid; geometry and properties are in `config/aois.geojson`.

**Aridity.** Following the UNCCD definition (annual precipitation ÷ potential evapotranspiration between 0.05 and 0.65), the aridity index is mean annual CHIRPS v3 rainfall divided by mean annual Hobbins reference evapotranspiration, 1991–2020. Five AOIs are drylands (0.14–0.32, arid to semi-arid); the erg is hyper-arid (0.043) and is kept deliberately as a stable control.

**Seasons.** The wet season is the wettest four consecutive months of the 1991–2020 rainfall climatology and the dry season the driest four (`reports/figures/m1_aoi_climatology.png`). The Sahel sites are strongly seasonal (92–93 % of rain in the wet season), Kubuqi has summer monsoon rain (77 %), and the Aral Sea and Lake Urmia receive winter–spring rain. At the erg, rain is too scarce for seasons to matter.

**Sentinel-2 availability.** Every candidate exceeds the target of 20 usable acquisition dates per year in every year 2019–2025 (a date is usable if a product covering the AOI that day has tile cloud cover of at most 40 %). The minimum per AOI ranges from 47 (the two Sahel sites) to 126 (the erg); counts rise in 2025 when Sentinel-2C joined (`reports/figures/m1_aoi_availability.png`).

**Imagery check.** Dry-season true-colour images for 2019 and 2025 (`reports/figures/m1_aoi_true_color.png`) show exposed seabed at the Aral Sea, rangeland and laterite plateau at the two Sahel sites, and uniform dunes at the erg, as expected. Two AOIs were moved after this check. At its first position the Lake Urmia square was mostly lake (open water in 2019, salt flat in 2025: lake desiccation rather than land degradation); it was moved about 10 km west (37.7 N, 45.19 E) and now covers farmland and shore, with the receding lake on its eastern edge. The first Kubuqi square showed sand and a salt lake with no re-greening; it was moved to 40.19 N, 109.15 E, inside the Kubuqi Desert Closed Reserve in Dalat County, where shrub sand-fixation plantations have been studied (Guo et al., 2025), and now shows planted patches among dunes plus some centre-pivot cropland.
